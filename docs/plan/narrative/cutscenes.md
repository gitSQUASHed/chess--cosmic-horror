# Narrative and Cutscenes

## Story arc

The game begins as a mundane tabletop chess game. The king's world is rigid, rule-bound, and physically contained by the board. The players' need to clean up turns them into gods from the king's point of view. When the board falls away, the king enters another plane of existence where he is free from chess rules but has lost safety, clarity, and the borders of his world.

## Opening

The chess match has ended. The king and two surviving pieces exchange a small line about the game finally being over. A distant player says food is ready and the board must be cleared. A hand removes the two other pieces. It reaches for the king, but he dodges. Phase 1 begins.

## Board-collapse transition

At the end of Phase 1, tiles begin falling away. The king falls into void and the scene becomes dark. He lands later on the pillar field. A short line establishes relief at no longer being bound by the old rules.

## Textbox presentation

All cutscene and important story text uses one pixel-font textbox that covers the bottom part of the screen. The textbox pauses the cutscene or game until the player presses Space, or an equivalent confirm input. It intentionally gives the player a short rest:

- before Phase 1;
- between the two phases;
- during the final ending scene.

The textbox should be distinct from regular gameplay and should not become a persistent HUD element.
