# Movement

## Phase 1 — King movement

- The king moves exactly one board tile per move in any of eight directions.
- Board edges and active walls block movement.
- Movement is intentionally rigid: the player reads attacks and chooses a legal chess-like escape square.

## Phase 2 — Free movement

- The king moves freely across existing pillar surfaces.
- Entering a missing tile without jump protection causes the void ending.
- The player can initiate a jump-dash from a solid tile.
- A jump-dash provides a short burst of speed and can cross narrow void gaps.
- During a dash, steering is deliberately limited to a slow turn. A dash is therefore an evasive commitment, not a universal escape button.
- A dash may carry the king into a hole, into a wall, or toward a hand. The player must choose both its direction and its landing space.

## Counterplay intent

- Sharp regular movement changes exploit the slow high-speed turning of guiding missile hands.
- Jump-dashes create a short answer to space denial, but introduce route risk.
- Walls, holes, and persistent tile destruction should make prior movement choices affect later routes.
